Pragmatism
A philosophical tradition that views language and thought as tools for prediction, problem solving, and action, rather than describing, representing, or mirroring reality. Pragmatists contend that most philosophical topics—such as the nature of knowledge, language, concepts, meaning, belief, and science—are best viewed in terms of their practical uses and successes.
Dogma
Any belief held definitively and without the possibility of reform. It may be in the form of an official system of principles or doctrines (religion, philosophical school, postulates/axioms, systems).
Bureaucracy
A system of organization where laws or regulatory authority are implemented by civil servants, as opposed to elected officials. This can exist in either public or private-owned entities, most commonly divided amongst departments, with examples being corporations, societies, nonprofit organizations, and clubs.
Extradition
The formal legal process by which one jurisdiction surrenders a person accused or convicted of a crime to another jurisdiction where the crime occurred for the purposes of prosecution or punishment.
